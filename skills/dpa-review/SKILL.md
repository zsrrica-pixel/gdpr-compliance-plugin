# DPA / processor contract review

Establish roles first (controller, processor, or joint controllers; Art. 26 applies to the latter). Then mark each Art. 28(3) element Present / Weak / Missing, quoting the clause:

1. Subject matter, duration, nature and purpose of processing
2. Types of personal data and categories of data subjects
3. Processing only on documented controller instructions (incl. transfers)
4. Confidentiality commitments for personnel
5. Security measures (Art. 32), ideally in an annex
6. Sub-processors: prior authorisation (general or specific), flow-down of the same obligations, list and change notice with right to object
7. Assistance with data subject requests
8. Assistance with Arts. 32-36 (security, breach notice, DPIAs); breach notice to controller "without undue delay" with a concrete timeframe
9. Deletion or return of data at end of services
10. Audit and information rights

Also check: international transfer mechanism (SCCs module, adequacy, DPF) and transfer impact assessment, liability caps that undercut data protection duties, and consistency between the DPA and the main agreement (order of precedence).

Output a findings table, a risk rating, and proposed redline language for each gap. Working draft, not legal advice.

## Rubric and format
- **Present**: clause meets Art. 28 as written. **Weak**: clause exists but is vague or sets a non-compliant term (e.g. a 10-business-day breach notice). **Missing**: no clause.
- Findings table columns: #, element, status, clause quote/evidence. Put the "also check" items in a second table.
- Risk rating: **High** (any core Art. 28(3) element Missing, or no transfer mechanism), **Medium** (only Weak items), **Low** (all Present, minor wording).
- If roles are not stated, state your assumption. If the main agreement is not supplied, say precedence could not be checked.
- Redline benchmarks are suggestions: breach notice 24-72h from awareness, 30 days' sub-processor notice, deletion within 30 days. Mark them as negotiable positions.
